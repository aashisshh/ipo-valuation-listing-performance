Does heavy subscription buy a bigger listing pop?

Yes on day one, no after that. IPOs subscribed 100x or more closed about 40% above issue price on average, and issues under 10x closed flat. But subscription shows no reliable link to what the stock does in the months that follow.

What the regression says

Model A

R² 0.34

listing gain ~ ln(total subscription) + ln(issue size) Both terms are significant, but about two thirds of the variation stays unexplained.

Model B

R² 0.45

listing gain ~ ln(QIB) + ln(NII) + ln(retail) + ln(issue size) Splitting demand by investor type adds explanatory power. QIB, NII and retail subscription correlate at 0.6 to 0.85, so read the coefficients together, not one by one.

Limits of this study

  • One half-year. Aug to Dec 2025 only. It says nothing about other market regimes.
  • No valuation multiples. P/E and P/B need prospectus financials that the source does not carry. The next step is adding them for the largest issues.
  • Snapshot returns. Return till date is a single Nov 2026 reading, so August listings have had longer to move than December ones.
  • Association, not cause. Strong demand and a strong pop move together here; the data cannot say which drives which.

Data: Trendlyne IPO screener (Mainboard 2025), pulled Nov 2026. Two trust listings (Knowledge Realty Trust, Anantam Highways InvIT) are excluded because they have no comparable retail book. Listing gain is computed as listing-day close divided by issue price, minus 1.

Tools: Excel, SQL (SQLite) and Python (pandas, statsmodels). Code and workbook: github.com/aashisshh/ipo-valuation-listing-performance
